# Montréal à double entente — identity brief

**Brand name selected by Martin:** Montréal à double entente

**Signature:** Secrets de l'Est, lanes from the West

**Naming decision:** The public brand remains **Montréal à double entente**. **Montréal à double sens** is the title of Bernardo’s first-person manifesto, where it works as an editorial idea rather than a competing brand name.

The French name and bilingual signature stay identical across the French, English and Spanish interfaces. Bernardo remains the named guide. This naming decision supersedes the candidates in the supplied “Bernardo Nieuwland: Brand Brief and Identity Directions”; that source document is retained as background.

## Strategic brief

- **Audience:** Visitors planning a Montréal visit, with French and English as the usual tour languages and Spanish available for some dates, plus private groups who want a route shaped around their interests. Assumption: the visitor often chooses between a short overview and a deeper neighbourhood walk before arriving in the city.
- **Offer:** Collective walks of 2, 3 or 4 hours; custom private walks; Lachine, francisation, family, accessibility, contribution-based, institutional and digital formats, each marked as available, occasional or in development. The offer is a menu that adjusts route, pace and depth to the audience.
- **Differentiation:** Bernardo starts with what is visible in the street, then connects it to the histories, institutions and rules meeting there. He calls these encounters Montréal’s “solitudes.”
- **Buying context:** Visitors need to see duration, route and language quickly, then ask about dates or plan a private route. The site should make those next steps obvious without inventing prices or schedules.
- **Trust requirement:** Place the APGT certification and Bernardo’s geographer profile beside his introduction. Keep route conditions and developing formats explicit.
- **Brand adjectives:** Curious, perceptive, animated, warm, intellectually playful.
- **Avoid:** Generic tourist-brochure polish, textbook-like exposition, academic jargon, constant annotations, overstated access promises and too many examples before a visitor chooses a tour.
- **Visual thesis:** Montréal à double entente invites a second reading of the city. Begin with a place visitors recognize, then let street-level details and occasional paired images reveal the tension Bernardo notices. The placeholder mark turns that idea into a compact signal: four rounded fields meet at a gold crossing, suggesting separate routes that share one city.

## Website branding brief

- **Hero mood:** A real Montréal street view headed “Montréal à double entente”, followed by the signature “Secrets de l'Est, lanes from the West”. State the language pattern plainly—French and English most often, Spanish on some dates—and offer direct paths to collective and private tours.
- **Hierarchy:** Hero → Bernardo and APGT credential → three duration-led collective cards → compact audience-led offer matrix → private-tour callout → practical FAQ → Instagram and field notes.
- **Typography:** Use a characterful serif for large headlines, a calm sans serif for paragraphs and routes, and a restrained monospaced face for small labels and durations.
- **Palette:** Midnight navy and deep teal create the urban field; warm gold carries the crossing line and calls to action; blue, red, green and violet distinguish the four route families without turning the interface into a map legend.
- **Layout rhythm:** Keep the broad image and the guide portrait as the two largest visual moments. Let the tour cards carry the decision; keep supporting sections lighter.
- **Trust architecture:** APGT appears with Bernardo’s introduction. Each standard card states its duration, route and use of transit where relevant. The audience-led matrix distinguishes available, occasional and developing formats instead of presenting every idea as bookable. The FAQ repeats those boundaries.
- **Image logic:** Use a wide street scene to orient, Bernardo’s own portrait to establish who leads the walk, and close details for the tour cards and field notes. Pair images when a comparison carries the story.
- **Interaction tone:** Plain navigation, calm hover states and clear actions. Use questions to invite attention; keep the longer historical account in notes and tour descriptions.

## Mini style guide

- **Primary mark:** The placeholder four-field symbol sits beside the “Montréal à double entente” wordmark, with Montréal above à double entente in compact headers. A small “Avec Bernardo” / “With Bernardo” / “Con Bernardo” byline identifies the guide. Keep the mark un-cropped and give it room to read at small sizes.
- **Alternate mark:** The full “Montréal à double entente” name in compact utility type for footers. Use the bilingual signature beneath the name in the homepage hero and editorial introductions.
- **Palette:** Midnight `#0B1728`; deep teal `#122C3D`; warm off-white `#F8F3E8`; gold `#F3B62E`; route blue `#2E6093`; route red `#A32334`; route green `#25786D`; route violet `#70418E`.
- **Typography:** Headlines in Georgia or the system’s editorial serif; body in Avenir Next / Avenir with system sans-serif fallbacks; utility labels in SF Mono or Consolas.
- **Spacing rules:** Keep the main measure near 1,160 px; use generous section breaks; align tour-card copy and route notes to the left; keep photo credits small and unobtrusive.
- **Do:** Show actual places and Bernardo; explain one “solitude” in plain language; use a detail or question to reward a second look; keep APGT easy to find.
- **Don’t:** Turn each page into a collage; decorate every image with lines; present developing formats as bookable; make the visual system feel like a history textbook or a generic tourism campaign.

## Placeholder logo direction

The logo is a four-part crossing rather than a literal landmark. Its rounded lobes can read as neighbourhoods, languages, visitors and histories meeting at a shared centre. The gold structure is the strongest cue, so it should appear in the navigation state, primary action and one quiet rule—not as a border around every card. The four interior colours can identify tour families, but the copy remains the decision aid: duration, route, language and what the visitor will notice.

Use the mark as a welcome signal and a faint watermark, never as a repeated sticker. On photography, preserve the full silhouette and avoid placing it over busy details. In small contexts, use the symbol alone; in editorial contexts, pair it with the name and the bilingual signature.

## Voice pattern

**Notice → ask → reveal.** Start with a concrete detail or a curious question, then give a short, welcoming explanation. Save the full historical account for tour pages and blog posts.

**Signature:** Secrets de l'Est, lanes from the West

Keep this exact bilingual wording in every language. “Double entente” carries the idea of several readings and a possible meeting between perspectives. The East/West line adds rhythm and a walking cue; it does not create two new tour products. Practical tour descriptions and navigation remain translated.
